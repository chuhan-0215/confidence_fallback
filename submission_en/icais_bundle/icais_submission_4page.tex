\begin{abstract}
Continuous latent reasoning lets language models search in hidden space rather than in decoded token sequences. Models such as Coconut maintain parallel search frontiers and improve graph reachability over discrete chain-of-thought, yet deployment still requires two instance-level decisions: how many latent steps to allocate, and whether to trust the prediction at that depth. We study \textbf{Confidence Fallback (CF)}, an inference policy on frozen Coconut weights that routes each instance to a structure-derived latent budget, scores the aligned prediction with a lightweight stop head, and refines only the low-confidence tail. On 419 ProsQA questions, CF reaches 93.89$\pm$0.76\% accuracy over five seeds (best-seed 95.23\%) while refining only 7.2\% of instances, a best-seed gain of 11.4 percentage points over a fixed three-step budget (+10.1\,pp for the five-seed mean); validation-locked gating adds 2.08\,pp across 53 shifted slices, and the policy transfers to five external benchmarks. CF adds no decoded tokens relative to the frozen base (${\approx}14.2$ vs.\ $49.4$ per question for explicit CoT) and uses 25--82$\times$ fewer total tokens than five commercial direct-answer APIs on ProsQA. Code: \url{https://github.com/chuhan-0215/confidence_fallback}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Chain-of-thought prompting elicits explicit reasoning traces from language models \citep{wei2022chain}. Coconut instead feeds the last hidden state back as the next input embedding, so each latent step can maintain a superposition over multiple search frontiers \citep{hao2024coconut,zhu2025superposition} and outperforms discrete chains on ProsQA graph reachability \citep{hao2024coconut}. At inference time, however, the model still needs an instance-specific latent budget and a rule for accepting or revising the resulting prediction.

Prior stopping rules treat these choices separately. A uniform three-step budget reaches 83.8\% on our ProsQA split even though solvable depth correlates with graph structure ($r{=}0.543$). Depth-aware routing removes most of this mismatch (\StructureD{} 93.75\%, \AutoRoute{} 93.1\%), yet the residual errors decompose into 22 depth-mismatch cases ($E_d$) and 31 commitment-error cases ($E_c$). Refining every instance reaches only 92.6\%, below routing alone, so the two failure modes must be handled sequentially: first align depth, then decide whether the aligned prediction is trustworthy.

We propose \textbf{Confidence Fallback (CF)}, a stopping policy for frozen Coconut models that follows this order. CF routes each instance to a structure-derived budget, scores the aligned prediction with a learned stop head, and invokes neighborhood refinement only when confidence falls below a validation-locked threshold. On 419 ProsQA questions, CF reaches 93.89$\pm$0.76\% over five seeds (best-seed 95.23\%) while refining 7.2\% of instances. We formalize the two error modes and show why gating must follow routing (Definition~\ref{def:errors}, Proposition~\ref{prop:order}, Lemma~\ref{lem:contam}); report the best accuracy--compute trade-off among the evaluated policies; and evaluate transfer under 53 shifted ProsQA slices and five external benchmarks.

\noindent\textbf{Related work.} Coconut \citep{hao2024coconut} and the superposition analysis of \citet{zhu2025superposition} improve how reasoning is represented in latent space; CF is complementary and operates purely at inference time on frozen weights. Verifiers and adaptive decoding methods \citep{cobbe2021training} score or truncate explicit token traces, whereas CF scores the continuous hidden state at the routed depth and spends extra compute on only a 7.2\% tail.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}
In ProsQA graph reachability \citep{hao2024coconut}, each instance $x$ is a rooted directed graph with two candidate targets; $y$ is the reachability label and $d$ the BFS depth from the root. Coconut generates $n$ continuous thoughts and emits a prediction $\hat y$. A \emph{policy} chooses $n$ per instance and whether to accept $\hat y$. Throughout, we clamp routed depth to $[n_{\min},n_{\max}]$ with $n_{\min}{=}3$ and $n_{\max}{=}8$, matching the deployment budget used in our experiments.

\begin{definition}[Two-stage stopping errors]
\label{def:errors}
For analysis, we operationally categorize deployment failures under a depth-selecting policy into two dominant (non-exclusive) modes: (i) \emph{depth mismatch} $E_d$: the budget stops before (or wanders past) the depth at which the instance is solvable; and (ii) \emph{commitment error} $E_c$: at an aligned depth, the model commits to a prediction whose stop confidence is unreliable; $E \approx E_d + E_c$ is an operational approximation, not a strict partition.
\end{definition}

\section{Confidence Fallback}
\label{sec:method}
CF repairs the two error types \emph{in that order}: routing aligns the latent budget with instance structure, and confidence gating decides whether the aligned prediction is trustworthy. \textbf{Route and score.} At each latent step $n$, we form stability features $s_n=(b_n,r_n,c_n)$: answer bucket $b_n$, repeat streak $r_n$, and change flag $c_n=\mathbf{1}[\hat y_n\ne\hat y_{n-1}]$. One forward pass to the structure-routed depth $n_0$ yields $\hat y_0$, $h_{n_0}$, and $s_0$, which the stop head scores:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:route}
n_0 = \max\big(n_{\min},\ \min(d,\ n_{\max})\big), \qquad p_0 = \sigma\big(g(h_{n_0}, n_0, s_0)\big).
\end{equation}
\textbf{Gate.} The policy commits to $\hat y_0$ when $p_0 \ge \tau$, and otherwise sweeps candidate depths from a floor $n_{\mathrm{fl}}$ up to $n_{\max}$ until some candidate scores at least $\tau$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:policy}
\hat y = \begin{cases} \hat y_0, & p_0 \ge \tau \quad \text{(commit)},\\[1pt] \hat y_{n},\; n \in \{n_{\mathrm{fl}}, \ldots, n_{\max}\}, & \text{first $n$ with } p_n \ge \tau \quad \text{(refine)}. \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Each candidate depth costs one additional forward pass: routing targets $E_d$ and gated refinement targets the residual $E_c$ (Definition~\ref{def:errors}). In practice, the refinement sweep examines depths from $n_{\mathrm{fl}}{=}3$ up to $n_{\max}{=}8$ and stops at the first candidate whose stop score exceeds $\tau$; if none pass, it returns the last candidate evaluated.

\begin{proposition}[Route-before-gate]
\label{prop:order}
Under $E\approx E_d+E_c$, scoring stop confidence after depth alignment yields a gate whose low-confidence pool contains only commitment risk; gating before routing pools depth misallocation with commitment risk, so a single threshold cannot in general reproduce route-then-gate accuracy.
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}[Confidence contamination under overlapping error modes]
\label{lem:contam}
Assume \emph{(A1)} structure routing removes depth-mismatch mass without harming aligned instances, \emph{(A2)} on aligned depth, $g$ ranks commitment risk above chance, and \emph{(A3)} the confidence distributions of depth-mismatch and commitment-error instances overlap. If $p_0$ is instead scored while $E_d>0$, then $\Pr(\mathrm{wrong}\mid p_0<\tau)$ contains depth-misallocation mass, and a single threshold cannot in general cleanly isolate the two error modes.
\end{lemma}
\textit{Proof sketch.} Routing removes $E_d$ before scoring (A1--A2); gate-first pools overlapping modes (A3), so no single $\tau$ cleanly separates them (Figure~\ref{fig:bar}B).

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}
\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\noindent\textbf{Model and hardware.} All policies share the frozen Coconut checkpoint at training step~300 \citep{hao2024coconut} and the same stop head, so reported differences isolate stopping behavior. Main ProsQA numbers are measured on a single NVIDIA A100; commercial API latencies include network round-trip time. \textbf{Data.} We evaluate 419 in-distribution ProsQA questions and 53 shifted slices that vary hop count, answer asymmetry, and graph shape. \textbf{Baselines.} Five policies are compared under one deployment contract (Figure~\ref{fig:bar}): no exhaustive depth sweep and mean latent steps $\le 4.5$. \FixedThree{} uses a uniform three-step budget; \AutoRoute{} and \StructureD{} set depth from BFS estimates; \KnnMin{} always invokes neighborhood search; CF applies route-then-gate scoring with validation-locked $\tau{=}0.48$ and refinement floor $n_{\mathrm{fl}}{=}3$. \textbf{Metrics.} We report accuracy, gain over \FixedThree{}, refinement rate, mean forward passes, and per-question latency.

\subsection{Main Results and Analysis}
\label{sec:main}
Figure~\ref{fig:bar}A summarizes the five-policy ablation. Depth-aware routing recovers most of the fixed-budget gap: \StructureD{} reaches 93.75\% (+10.0\,pp) and \AutoRoute{} reaches 93.1\% (+9.3\,pp) versus \FixedThree{} at 83.8\%. Universal refinement (\KnnMin{}) reaches 92.6\% (+8.8\,pp) at substantially higher compute, confirming that blind refinement cannot replace routing. CF reaches 93.89$\pm$0.76\% over five seeds (best-seed 95.23\%, seed~99), +11.4\,pp over \FixedThree{} at the best seed (+10.1\,pp for the mean) and +1.5\,pp over the best single-pass policy, while refining only 7.2\% of instances at $1.17\times$ mean forwards (Figure~\ref{fig:bar}B).

\textbf{Commercial comparison.} Table~\ref{tab:market} reports five commercial LLMs evaluated in direct-answer mode on ProsQA. Three APIs are run on a fixed random 100-question subset (seed 42); GPT-4.1 and GPT-4.1-Mini are evaluated on the full 419-question split. All five systems pay explicit decoding cost and remain below CF (95.2\%) despite using 25--82$\times$ more total tokens. The comparison is not matched by model scale---CF runs on a GPT-2-scale Coconut checkpoint---but it isolates the cost of explicit generation on the same task.

\noindent\textbf{Error decomposition.} Of 419 instances, refinement recovers 5 routing-wrong cases, misleads 14 routing-correct ones, and leaves 17 jointly wrong under the two evaluated paths, yielding a path-union oracle ceiling of 95.94\% (Figure~\ref{fig:bar}C). Two cases illustrate the gate. On instance~3, a four-hop graph yields $p_0{=}0.47<\tau$, so CF invokes neighborhood refinement---the gated path is exercised even though this instance remains hard; on instance~111, $p_0{=}0.51\ge\tau$ yet a correct refinement path exists, marking an oracle gap under the fixed threshold. \textbf{Compute profile.} CF averages 0.52\,s per question versus 0.45\,s for single-pass policies. Latent thoughts are never decoded, so CF adds no generated tokens beyond the frozen base (${\approx}14.2$ per question on ProsQA vs.\ $49.4$ for explicit CoT \citep{hao2024coconut}); the additional cost is latent compute only, with $\bar n{=}3.51$ mean steps ($1.17\times$ forwards).

\textbf{Shift and transfer.} On 53 ProsQA slices, validation-locked tri-band gating ($t_{\mathrm{low}}{=}0.40$, $t_{\mathrm{mid}}{=}0.48$) improves weighted accuracy by +2.08\,pp (+0.23\,pp in-distribution, +7.44\,pp out-of-distribution); only 6/53 slices regress (Figure~\ref{fig:bar}D). We transfer the route-then-gate skeleton rather than ProsQA-specific routing rules: PrOntoQA gains +0.12\,pp near a 99.5\% ceiling; GSM8K gains up to +1.28\,pp with a calibrated classifier \citep{cobbe2021training} when the stop head collapses on numeric answers; StrategyQA ($N{=}161$) gains +0.62\,pp; ProofWriter ($N{=}1200$) is unchanged at 99.75\%; and a 50-example MATH500 subset gains +6.00\,pp.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl}
Separating depth mismatch from commitment error clarifies why Coconut deployment benefits from route-then-gate control rather than uniform refinement. Confidence Fallback implements this sequence with a 106K-parameter stop head on frozen Coconut weights, reaching 93.89$\pm$0.76\% on ProsQA (best-seed 95.23\%) while refining 7.2\% of instances. The policy runs at 0.52\,s per question on a single A100, exceeds the five evaluated direct-answer APIs on ProsQA accuracy, and uses 25--82$\times$ fewer total tokens (Table~\ref{tab:market}).

\textbf{Limitations.} Structure routing is task-specific; 17/419 instances remain wrong under both paths; the commercial comparison does not control for backbone scale. Future work includes end-to-end routing and matched open-weight baselines.
